Sweden · 1960–1970

The Saab 35 Draken, entering frontline service in 1960, pushed that self-sufficient tradition into the supersonic era, distinguished by an unusual "double delta" wing designed to combine high speed with manageable handling at the lower speeds needed to operate from Sweden's network of dispersed airfields. Unlike the Tunnan, the Draken did find export customers — Denmark, Finland, and Austria all bought the type, making it Sweden's most internationally successful fighter design to that point.

Saab also expanded into trainer aircraft with the Saab 105, developed as a private venture rather than to a government specification and adopted by the Swedish Air Force in 1967; it would remain in service for 57 years, one of the longest operational lives of any aircraft covered on this site.

Aircraft types covered

  • Saab 35 Draken Military — Supersonic interceptor and fighter
  • Saab 105 Military — Twin-jet trainer and light attack aircraft
