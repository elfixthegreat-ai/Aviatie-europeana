Lockheed F-104G Starfighter (German-built)

Manufacturer: Originally Lockheed (USA); built under license in West Germany chiefly by Messerschmitt, with Dornier
Country / decade: West Germany, 1960–1970
Category: Military
Role: Supersonic interceptor and strike fighter
First flight: Type first flew 1954 (USA); German-built aircraft from early 1960s
Entered service: 1960 (F-104F trainer); main F-104G variant from 1962

Design and development

The original American F-104 was designed as a lightweight, high-altitude interceptor optimised almost entirely for speed, with famously small, thin wings; the "G" model built under European license was substantially reworked for the very different mission NATO air forces actually needed — low-level strike and reconnaissance in Central Europe — with a stronger airframe, terrain-following radar, and different avionics from the original American version. That mismatch between the aircraft's original design intent and its adapted NATO role is often cited as a contributing factor in the type's high accident rate in German service.

Where it was used, and by whom

The West German Luftwaffe was the largest single operator of the Starfighter worldwide, acquiring around 916 aircraft — roughly a third of total worldwide production — for both the Luftwaffe and the naval air arm, the Marineflieger.

Notes

Although designed in the United States, the F-104G was built under a multinational European licensing consortium including Messerschmitt (later part of MBB), Dornier, Fiat in Italy, Fokker in the Netherlands, and SABCA in Belgium — included here as a German-manufactured aircraft. Its high accident rate in German service earned it the nickname “Witwenmacher” (Widowmaker).
