PZL TS-11 Iskra

Manufacturer: PZL-Mielec
Country / decade: Poland, 1960–1970
Category: Military
Role: Jet trainer
First flight: 5 February 1960
Entered service: 1964, with the Polish Air Force

Design and development

PZL-Mielec's engineers designed the Iskra with its own domestically developed jet engine, the PZL SO-1 (later SO-3), rather than relying on a Soviet or Warsaw Pact power plant — a rare instance of a Warsaw Pact country fielding a combat aircraft powered by an engine of entirely its own design. That level of self-sufficiency, even within an alliance built around centralised Soviet supply, mirrors the same instinct that led Poland to keep developing the Iskra even after the Warsaw Pact had already chosen a different aircraft as its common standard trainer.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Polish Air Force and Polish Navy were the sole military operators within the Warsaw Pact — Poland chose to keep its own design after the L-29 was selected as the bloc-wide standard. India later bought 76 aircraft in the 1970s and 1990s.

Notes

The Iskra was Poland's first domestically designed jet aircraft, powered by an equally home-grown engine, the PZL SO-1/SO-3. It remained in Polish military service for more than 50 years, into the 2020s.
