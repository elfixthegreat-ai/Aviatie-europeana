Poland · 1960–1970

Poland's aviation industry was, by volume, one of the most productive in the entire Warsaw Pact: according to the Polish Air Force Association's official historian, Polish factories built around 10,000 aircraft between 1950 and 1970, approaching an output of 1,000 aircraft a year at the Cold War's height. Much of that output was licensed Soviet material — PZL-Mielec became the sole worldwide source for the Soviet-designed An-2 biplane and built the MiG-15 and MiG-17 under license as the basis of the Polish Air Force's frontline strength.

Poland's clearest departure from that pattern of Soviet licensing was the PZL TS-11 Iskra, a domestically designed jet trainer first flown in 1960. Poland pursued the Iskra even after the Warsaw Pact selected Czechoslovakia's rival L-29 Delfín as its bloc-wide standard trainer in 1961 — making Poland the only Warsaw Pact member to field its own trainer rather than adopt the common type. In an unusual cross-bloc detail, the Iskra's first prototype flew with an engine supplied by non-aligned Yugoslavia, since Cold War politics made buying the preferred British engine directly impossible.

The PZL brand covered a genuinely large industrial base — airframe plants at Mielec and Warsaw-Okęcie, helicopter production at Świdnik, and engine works at Rzeszów and Kalisz — employing tens of thousands of workers across the country.
