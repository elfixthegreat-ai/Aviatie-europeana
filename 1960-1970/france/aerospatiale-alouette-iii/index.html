Aérospatiale Alouette III

Manufacturer: Sud Aviation / Aérospatiale
Country / decade: France, 1960–1970
Category: Civil & military
Role: Light utility helicopter
First flight: 28 February 1959
Entered service: 1960–61

Design and development

The Alouette III was developed as a more powerful successor to Sud Aviation's earlier Alouette II, adopting a slightly larger cabin able to carry up to seven people while keeping the same turbine-engine simplicity that had made the original such a reliable design. Its record-setting high-altitude trials in the Himalayas were not just a publicity exercise: they directly demonstrated the aircraft's suitability for mountain rescue and military operations at altitudes that piston-engined helicopters of the era struggled to reach at all.

Where it was used, and by whom

The French Army placed an early order, but the first customers were actually export sales. The Alouette III went on to serve more than 100 operators worldwide, including the Swiss Army (which also license-built 60 under Eidgenössische Flugzeugwerke), the Portuguese Air Force (142 aircraft, used into colonial-era Africa), and India, where Hindustan Aeronautics license-built over 300 as the HAL Chetak.

Notes

Over 2,000 Alouette IIIs were eventually built, including licensed production in India, Romania, and Switzerland, making it one of the most widely used light helicopters of the period. It earned a strong reputation for high-altitude performance, including a demonstration landing above 6,000 metres in the Himalayas.
