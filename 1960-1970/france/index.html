France · 1960–1970

Dassault Aviation turned the Mirage III into one of the most commercially successful fighter programmes in the world during this decade, selling to air forces from Israel to Australia to South Africa — sales that helped make France one of the world's largest arms exporters by the early 1970s. That independent export drive matched France's broader foreign policy under President Charles de Gaulle, who withdrew French forces from NATO's integrated military command in 1966 while remaining a treaty member, a stance that put a premium on France having combat aircraft it did not depend on allies to supply.

Sud Aviation's helicopter arm added a second success story: the Alouette III, introduced in 1959–61, became one of the most widely used light helicopters anywhere, prized for its unusually strong high-altitude performance and eventually built under license in India, Romania, and Switzerland as well as France.

Meanwhile, the Concorde treaty signed in 1962 moved from paperwork toward hardware, with the first prototype due to fly by the end of the decade — a long-term commitment that would only pay off, commercially and symbolically, in the 1970s.
