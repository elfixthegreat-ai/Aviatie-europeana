1960–1970

The 1960s were the high-water mark of independent national fighter design in Europe, producing supersonic types such as Britain's Lightning, France's Mirage III, and Sweden's Draken, each built largely without foreign partners and, in the French and Swedish cases, sold in large numbers to air forces well outside Europe. NATO also made its first serious effort at alliance-wide standardisation with the NBMR-1 competition, won by Italy's Fiat G.91, though most member states ultimately still bought national designs. On the civil side, the short-haul jet airliner matured, led by France's Caravelle and, from the end of the decade, the Netherlands' Fokker F28. The first concrete steps toward international collaboration were also taken in this decade, most notably the 1962 Anglo-French treaty that launched the Concorde programme.
