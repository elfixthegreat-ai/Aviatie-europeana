Czechoslovakia · 1960–1970

Czechoslovak industry operated on a fundamentally different model from Western Europe's competing national manufacturers: as a member of the Warsaw Pact and the COMECON economic bloc, Aero Vodochody built aircraft intended from the start for the whole Eastern Bloc rather than for Czechoslovakia's domestic air force alone.

The clearest expression of that model was the L-29 Delfín, first flown in 1959 — Czechoslovakia's first indigenously designed jet aircraft, and the winner of a formal 1961 fly-off competition against Poland's TS-11 Iskra and the Soviet Yak-30 to become the standard jet trainer for the entire Warsaw Pact. Every member air force except Poland adopted it, with the majority of the roughly 3,600 built going to the Soviet Union itself.

That outcome meant a huge share of an entire generation of Warsaw Pact pilots — quite possibly the largest single group of military pilots trained on one aircraft type anywhere in the Cold War — learned to fly on a Czechoslovak-built jet, a scale of influence far beyond what Czechoslovakia's own air force alone would have required.
