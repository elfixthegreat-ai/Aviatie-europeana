Aero L-29 Delfín

Manufacturer: Aero Vodochody
Country / decade: Czechoslovakia, 1960–1970
Category: Military
Role: Jet trainer
First flight: 5 April 1959
Entered service: 1963

Design and development

Aero's design team, led by Zdeněk Rublič and Karel Tomáš, built the first L-29 prototype around a British Bristol Siddeley Viper engine before switching production aircraft to a Czechoslovak-designed M701 engine — a sequence that let the programme fly and prove its airframe quickly using an available Western engine while Czechoslovak industry finished developing its own turbojet in parallel. The aircraft's straightforward, easy-to-maintain design was a deliberate choice aimed at air forces with far less technical infrastructure than Czechoslovakia's own, since it was always intended for export across the wider Warsaw Pact.

Where it was used, and by whom

Chosen in 1961 over Poland's TS-11 and the Soviet Yak-30 as the standard jet trainer for the Warsaw Pact, the L-29 was adopted by every member air force except Poland, with more than 2,000 of the roughly 3,600 built going to the Soviet Union. Export customers outside the bloc included Egypt, which flew armed L-29s in the 1973 Yom Kippur War.

Notes

The L-29 was Czechoslovakia's first indigenously designed and built jet aircraft, and remains one of the most-produced jet trainers in aviation history.
