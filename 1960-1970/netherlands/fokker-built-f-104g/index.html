Lockheed F-104G Starfighter (Fokker-built)

Manufacturer: Originally Lockheed (USA); built under license in the Netherlands by Fokker
Country / decade: Netherlands, 1960–1970
Category: Military
Role: Supersonic interceptor and strike fighter
First flight: Type first flew 1954 (USA); Dutch-built aircraft from the mid-1960s
Entered service: 1962, with the Royal Netherlands Air Force

Design and development

Fokker's share of F-104G production gave Dutch industry direct experience with the demanding manufacturing tolerances of a Mach 2 fighter, a step up in technical sophistication from the turboprop airliner work the company was simultaneously carrying out with the F27 — evidence that Dutch industry, like West Germany's, used the Starfighter programme partly as a deliberate way to rebuild advanced aerospace manufacturing skill alongside its civil business.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Royal Netherlands Air Force operated the F-104G as its principal strike fighter through the 1960s and 1970s. Fokker was one of five European companies (alongside Messerschmitt and Dornier in West Germany, Fiat in Italy, and SABCA in Belgium) licensed to build the type for NATO air forces.

Notes

Included here as a Dutch-manufactured aircraft, though the design originated with Lockheed in the United States. Fokker's involvement placed Dutch industry inside the largest multinational fighter-production programme of the 1960s.
