Italy · 1960–1970

Aermacchi built on Italy's reputation with the MB-326 jet trainer, first flown in 1957 and in Italian Air Force service from 1962; over the following decades it would be built under license and exported to customers on four continents, from Australia to Brazil to South Africa, making Italy one of the world's leading suppliers of jet trainers by volume rather than of frontline fighters.

Italian industry also diversified into rotorcraft: Agusta's licensed production of Bell's utility helicopters, beginning in the mid-1960s, gave Italy a genuine domestic helicopter-manufacturing base that would prove just as commercially durable as its fixed-wing exports. Meanwhile the G.91 continued in frontline Luftwaffe and Portuguese Air Force service throughout the decade, the latter flying it extensively during Portugal's colonial wars in Africa.

Aircraft types covered

  • Aermacchi MB-326 Military (trainer / light attack); limited civil use — Jet trainer and light attack aircraft
  • Agusta-Bell AB 204 / AB 205 Civil & military — Utility transport helicopter
