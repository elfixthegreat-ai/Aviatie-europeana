Aermacchi MB-326

Manufacturer: Aermacchi
Country / decade: Italy, 1960–1970
Category: Military (trainer / light attack); limited civil use
Role: Jet trainer and light attack aircraft
First flight: 10 December 1957
Entered service: February 1962, with the Italian Air Force

Design and development

Aermacchi designed the MB-326 around a straightforward configuration — straight wings, tandem seating, a single small turbojet — deliberately prioritising low operating cost and ease of maintenance over outright performance, reasoning correctly that air forces needed inexpensive trainers in large numbers far more than they needed sophisticated ones. That philosophy proved right commercially: the MB-326's licensed production in Australia, Brazil, and South Africa made it one of the most geographically dispersed manufacturing programmes of any aircraft covered on this site.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Italian Air Force was the first customer; Italy's flag carrier Alitalia even used a handful of unarmed MB-326s for pilot training. Beyond Italy, the type was built under license and exported to Australia, Brazil (as the Embraer EMB-326/AT-26 Xavante), and South Africa (as the Atlas Impala), and flew with more than ten air forces across four continents.

Notes

Over 800 MB-326s were built. South African Impalas saw combat in the Border War, and Argentine Navy examples were on standby during the 1982 Falklands conflict.
