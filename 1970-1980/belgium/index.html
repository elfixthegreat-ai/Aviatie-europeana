Belgium · 1970–1980

Belgian industry's role in this period was overwhelmingly one of license production rather than original design: companies like SABCA and Fairey Belgium built British and American types under license for the Belgian Air Force's own use, rather than developing indigenous combat aircraft the way Sweden or France did. That pattern reflected Belgium's position as a founding NATO member from 1949, with a defence policy built around continuous modernisation in step with the alliance rather than technological independence.

The clearest example in this decade was the Mirage 5BA: Belgium ordered 106 examples of Dassault's ground-attack variant of the Mirage III in 1968, built domestically by SABCA rather than imported directly from France, making Belgium the largest single operator of the Mirage 5 anywhere in the world.

That same "Northern Group" licensing model — Belgian, Dutch, and West German companies jointly building an aircraft designed elsewhere — had already been used for the F-104G Starfighter in the 1960s, and would be used again by Belgium at the end of the 1970s for the American F-16, suggesting a consistent Belgian industrial strategy across the whole period covered by this site.
