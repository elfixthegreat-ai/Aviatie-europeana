France · 1970–1980

Concorde's entry into commercial service in January 1976 was the decade's headline achievement, but it was only one part of a broader shift in how France organised its aerospace industry. In 1970, the state-owned Sud Aviation, Nord Aviation, and the missile firm SEREB merged into a single company, Aérospatiale — consolidating French industry in much the same way Britain had a decade earlier.

That new company became France's anchor partner in the founding of the Airbus consortium in 1970, and the first Airbus A300 — built jointly with West German, British, and Spanish partners — flew in 1972 and began reaching airlines in 1974. It marked a deliberate break from the purely national "flag carrier" model that had defined the previous two decades of European civil aviation.

Dassault, meanwhile, continued its independent fighter line with the Mirage F1, which entered French Air Force service in 1973 and extended the export success of the Mirage III into a new generation, even as the company's compatriots at Aérospatiale increasingly worked through multinational partnerships rather than going it alone.
