Fokker F28 Fellowship

Manufacturer: Fokker
Country / decade: Netherlands, 1970–1980
Category: Civil
Role: Twin-engine short-range jet airliner
First flight: 9 May 1967
Entered service: 28 March 1969, with Braathens SAFE (widest adoption through the 1970s)

Design and development

The F28's design deliberately reused as much of the F27's proven structural and systems knowledge as the switch to jet power allowed, letting Fokker develop a genuinely new aircraft type faster and at lower risk than starting entirely from scratch. Garuda Indonesia's decision to become its single largest operator reflected the same short-runway, regional-route logic that had made the F27 successful in similarly challenging operating environments a decade earlier.

Where it was used, and by whom

Garuda Indonesia became the largest operator with 62 aircraft; other major users included Braathens SAFE of Norway, Linjeflyg of Sweden, and Biman Bangladesh Airlines, alongside numerous regional carriers in South America and Scandinavia.

Notes

Developed as a jet-powered complement to the turboprop F27, the F28 was engineered to use roughly 85% of the same short or unprepared airfields, extending Fokker's regional-aircraft specialism into the jet era. A total of 241 were built.
