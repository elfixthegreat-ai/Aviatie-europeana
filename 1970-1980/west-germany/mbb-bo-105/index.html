MBB Bo 105

Manufacturer: Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm (MBB)
Country / decade: West Germany, 1970–1980
Category: Civil & military
Role: Light twin-engine multi-purpose helicopter
First flight: 16 February 1967
Entered service: 1970

Design and development

The Bo 105's hingeless rotor system, developed in-house by MBB rather than licensed from an established helicopter manufacturer, gave it manoeuvrability closer to a fixed-wing aircraft than a typical helicopter of its era, including the ability to perform loops and rolls — a genuine first for a certified civil helicopter. That technical achievement established MBB, previously known mainly for fixed-wing work, as a serious independent helicopter manufacturer in its own right.

Where it was used, and by whom

The West German armed forces were the largest operator, using around 200 as an anti-armour and liaison helicopter; the type was also widely sold to police, air rescue, and civil operators, and eventually to military and civil customers in around 55 countries, with additional manufacturing lines in Spain, Indonesia, and the Philippines.

Notes

The Bo 105 was the world's first light, twin-engine helicopter and the first with a rigid (hingeless) rotor system, which also made it the first helicopter certified to perform aerobatic manoeuvres such as loops.
