VFW-Fokker 614

Manufacturer: VFW-Fokker (West German–Dutch joint venture)
Country / decade: West Germany, 1970–1980
Category: Civil
Role: Twin-engine regional jet airliner
First flight: 14 July 1971
Entered service: August 1975, with Cimber Air

Design and development

The 614's most distinctive feature — engines mounted on pylons above the wing rather than below it or on the rear fuselage — was chosen to keep the engine intakes clear of runway debris on the short, sometimes unpaved airstrips the aircraft was meant to serve, and to simplify engine maintenance from ground level. The layout proved aerodynamically sound but commercially unpersuasive: with only 19 built, it remains one of the clearest examples on this site of a genuinely capable design failing commercially against better-established competitors.

Where it was used, and by whom

Commercial operators were limited to Cimber Air of Denmark, and Touraine Air Transport and Air Alsace of France; the West German Air Force also operated a small number. Total production was only 19 aircraft.

Notes

The VFW 614 was West Germany's first postwar jet airliner, notable for mounting its two engines on pylons above the wing rather than below it or on the tail. The programme struggled commercially and was cancelled in 1977; one aircraft continued flying as a research testbed for the German Aerospace Center (DLR) until 2012.
