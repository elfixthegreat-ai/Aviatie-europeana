Spain · 1970–1980

Spanish industry consolidated in 1972, when CASA absorbed Hispano Aviación — the same pattern of merger seen earlier in Britain, France, and Italy, though arriving somewhat later in Spain's case. The consolidated company's clearest success was an original design rather than a licensed one: the C-212 Aviocar, a rugged STOL transport that first flew in 1971 and entered Spanish Air Force service in 1974, going on to sell to roughly 90 operators worldwide.

Spain's broader political isolation began to ease across this decade, particularly after Franco's death in 1975, though the country would not join NATO until 1982. On the industrial side, CASA had already begun moving toward European integration before that wider political shift, becoming an early participant in the Airbus consortium from the early 1970s — placing Spanish industry, for the first time, inside the same collaborative framework as Britain, France, and West Germany.
