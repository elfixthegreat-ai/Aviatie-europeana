Saab 37 Viggen

Manufacturer: Saab AB
Country / decade: Sweden, 1970–1980
Category: Military
Role: Multirole combat aircraft (fighter, attack, reconnaissance, and trainer variants)
First flight: 8 February 1967
Entered service: 21 June 1971, with the Swedish Air Force

Design and development

The Viggen's canard-delta layout — a small foreplane ahead of the main delta wing — was chosen specifically to allow the very short landing rolls Sweden's road-base doctrine demanded, using the canard to generate extra lift at low speed without the complexity of the variable-sweep wings other countries were experimenting with for similar short-field requirements. Sweden's dispersed Bas 60 basing system, which the Viggen was purpose-built around, required pilots to land, refuel, and rearm at improvised road strips within minutes, using conscripted civilian ground crews trained specifically for that role.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Swedish Air Force was the sole operator, with 329 built across its variants. Sweden's policy of armed neutrality meant the Viggen was never exported, despite an unsuccessful 1970s bid for a United States Air Force fighter competition.

Notes

The Viggen was among the first production combat aircraft in the world to use a canard (delta-canard) configuration, and was designed to operate from short, dispersed road-based airstrips as part of Sweden's wartime basing doctrine.
