1970–1980

By the 1970s, European industrial collaboration had moved from treaty to hardware. Concorde entered scheduled supersonic passenger service in January 1976, jointly operated by Air France and British Airways, while the first Airbus A300s — built by a consortium of French, German, British, and Spanish companies — began reaching airlines from 1974 onward, establishing the model that would come to dominate European civil aviation. Military aviation saw the operational maturing of V/STOL technology in the Hawker Siddeley Harrier and further national combat aircraft such as Sweden's canard-winged Viggen and Italy's G222 tactical transport. Economic pressure and the 1973 oil crisis also ended several smaller national programmes, including West Germany's VFW 614 airliner, underscoring the case for shared development that Airbus was built on.
