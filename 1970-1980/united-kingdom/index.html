United Kingdom · 1970–1980

Concorde entered scheduled commercial service in January 1976, jointly operated by Air France and British Airways — a striking symbol of what joint Anglo-French investment could achieve, even if the aircraft would never be the commercial success either government had originally hoped for. Alongside it, the Hawker Siddeley Harrier brought a wholly British invention, vertical/short take-off and landing technology, into frontline NATO service, based in West Germany specifically so it could disperse away from vulnerable fixed runways in the event of a Warsaw Pact attack.

Industrial consolidation continued to its logical end point: in 1977 the government nationalised and merged BAC, Hawker Siddeley, and Scottish Aviation into a single company, British Aerospace, closing the era of competing national manufacturers that had defined Britain's — and much of Europe's — aircraft industry since the war.

The Harrier's export sale to the United States Marine Corps as the AV-8A was one of the few times in this period that a European-designed combat aircraft won a major American order, rather than the other way around, and it remains one of the decade's clearest British engineering successes.
