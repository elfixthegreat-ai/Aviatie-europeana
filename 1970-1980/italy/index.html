Italy · 1970–1980

Italian manufacturing consolidated in 1969, when Fiat Aviazione merged with Aerfer to form Aeritalia — echoing the same industrial logic that had already reshaped British, French, and German industry. The new company's first major programme was the G.222, a medium tactical transport that traced its origins to a 1962 NATO requirement for a vertical/short take-off transport, though the vertical-lift requirement was later dropped in favour of a more conventional STOL design.

Italy's largest commitment of the decade was its 15 percent share of the trinational Panavia Tornado programme alongside Britain and West Germany, for which Aeritalia built the aircraft's wings — a smaller stake than the two larger partners, reflecting Italy's smaller initial order, but still a genuine step into multinational combat-aircraft development rather than purely national or licensed production.

Aircraft types covered

  • Aeritalia G.222 Military — Medium short take-off and landing (STOL) tactical transport
  • Panavia Tornado Military — Variable-sweep-wing strike and multirole combat aircraft
