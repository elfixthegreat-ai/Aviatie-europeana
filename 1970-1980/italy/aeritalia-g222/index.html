Aeritalia G.222

Manufacturer: Aeritalia (formed from the 1969 merger of Fiat Aviazione and Aerfer)
Country / decade: Italy, 1970–1980
Category: Military
Role: Medium short take-off and landing (STOL) tactical transport
First flight: 18 July 1970
Entered service: April 1978, with the Italian Air Force

Design and development

The G.222 began life under a NATO requirement, NBMR-4, for a vertical/short take-off tactical transport, but as that ambitious requirement proved technically unworkable within the timeframe available, Aeritalia redesigned it as a more conventional, if still highly capable, short-field turboprop transport — a pragmatic retreat that ultimately produced a far more exportable aircraft than the original specification would likely have allowed. Its later adoption by the United States Air Force as the C-27A Spartan was a rare case of an Italian-designed military aircraft being bought directly by the American military.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Italian Air Force was the primary operator with 44 aircraft. The type was later exported to Argentina, Nigeria, and other air forces, and the United States purchased a small batch as the C-27A Spartan.

Notes

The G.222 originated in a 1962 NATO specification (NBMR-4) for a vertical/short take-off transport; the vertical-lift requirement was later dropped in favour of a conventional STOL design. It became the basis for the later, modernised C-27J Spartan.
