West Germany · 1950–1960

West Germany's aircraft industry did not exist for most of this decade. Allied occupation authorities had banned German aircraft manufacturing outright after the war, and it was only with West Germany's accession to NATO and the restoration of its sovereignty in May 1955 that the ban was lifted. The industry that restarted afterward did so cautiously and from close to zero, with none of the jet-fighter or airliner ambitions being pursued elsewhere in Western Europe.

Dornier's Do 27, first flown in June 1955, was the first aircraft built in quantity in West Germany after the war — a modest, rugged utility type rather than anything resembling the jets already flying in Britain and France. Tellingly, its design lineage traced back to Spain, where Claude Dornier's engineers had continued working during the years German manufacturing itself was forbidden, illustrating how thoroughly the German industry's continuity had been broken.

By the end of the decade West Germany had only just re-established a functioning aircraft industry, with full-scale rearmament and jet production still to come in the 1960s.
