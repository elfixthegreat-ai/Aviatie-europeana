Saab 29 Tunnan

Manufacturer: Saab (Svenska Aeroplan AB)
Country / decade: Sweden, 1950–1960
Category: Military
Role: Jet fighter — Sweden's first swept-wing jet design
First flight: 1 September 1948
Entered service: 1951, with the Swedish Air Force

Design and development

The Tunnan's distinctively rounded, barrel-shaped fuselage — the source of its nickname — was shaped around the engine and fuel tank layout Saab's engineers judged most aerodynamically efficient, even though it looked unconventional next to the slimmer fighters other countries were building at the same time. It was also Sweden's first attempt at a truly independent swept-wing jet design, built with aerodynamic data Saab partly derived from captured German wartime research, at a time when Sweden had no equivalent research establishment of its own to draw on.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Swedish Air Force was the sole major operator, with 661 built. The Austrian Air Force was the only export customer, operating 30 aircraft from 1961 to 1972.

Notes

Nicknamed the “Flying Barrel” for its rounded fuselage, the Tunnan saw combat during a United Nations peacekeeping mission in the Congo in the early 1960s and established the design tradition that Saab's later fighters would follow.
