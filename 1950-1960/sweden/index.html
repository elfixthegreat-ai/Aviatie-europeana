Sweden · 1950–1960

Sweden's policy of armed neutrality, maintained through both World Wars and the Cold War, meant Saab was expected to design and build combat aircraft entirely independently, without access to NATO's shared development programmes or the export markets that came with alliance membership. The Saab 29 Tunnan, Sweden's first swept-wing jet fighter, entered service in 1951 and became the backbone of that self-sufficient approach.

The Tunnan's only notable foreign deployment came through the United Nations rather than an alliance: Swedish Air Force examples saw combat during a UN peacekeeping mission in the Congo in the early 1960s, an unusual case of a neutral country's fighters seeing action abroad under an international rather than military-bloc mandate. Austria became the type's only export customer, buying 30 aircraft in 1961.

By the end of the decade, Sweden had proven that a country of its size could sustain a fully domestic fighter-design capability — a tradition Saab would continue for the rest of the Cold War.
