Switzerland · 1950–1960

Switzerland's neutrality, like Sweden's, created pressure for a self-sufficient aircraft industry, but Switzerland pursued that goal very differently. Three domestic manufacturers existed in this decade — the federal Eidgenössische Flugzeugwerke (F+W) at Emmen, Flug- und Fahrzeugwerke (FFA) at Altenrhein, and privately owned Pilatus at Stans — but with little cooperation between them, unlike the industrial consolidation happening elsewhere in Europe.

Switzerland's own attempts at an indigenous jet fighter, the F+W N-20 and the FFA P-16, both ran into serious technical and financial difficulties and were abandoned by 1959; Swiss policy leaned instead toward building foreign designs, such as the de Havilland Vampire and Venom, under license rather than risking further indigenous fighter programmes.

Pilatus took a different, ultimately more durable path, avoiding fighters altogether in favour of trainers and utility aircraft. Its PC-6 Porter, first flown in 1959, was designed for exceptional short-field performance and would remain in production for over sixty years — proof that Swiss industry's real strength in this period lay in rugged niche aircraft rather than in matching the fighter ambitions of its larger neighbours.
