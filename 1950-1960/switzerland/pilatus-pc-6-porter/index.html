Pilatus PC-6 Porter

Manufacturer: Pilatus Aircraft
Country / decade: Switzerland, 1950–1960
Category: Civil & military
Role: STOL utility aircraft
First flight: 4 May 1959
Entered service: early 1960s (widest adoption through the 1960s–70s)

Design and development

Pilatus built the Porter's wing and fuselage around a set of high-lift devices — full-span leading-edge slats and large flaps — specifically tuned for landing and taking off from short, rough mountain airstrips typical of the Swiss Alps, a design problem few other European manufacturers of the period were solving for at all. That very specific engineering focus, rather than any attempt to compete with larger transport aircraft, is what let the Porter remain competitive and in production for over sixty years.

Where it was used, and by whom

Operated by the Swiss Air Force and civil operators from the 1960s onward, the Porter went on to serve military and civil customers on every continent, including mountain and bush operators in the Alps, the Andes, and Africa; it was also license-built in the United States by Fairchild Hiller.

Notes

Renowned for exceptional short-field performance, the Porter remained in production for 60 years — an unusually long run for a single airframe design — with the last of roughly 604 aircraft delivered in 2022.
