Hispano Aviación HA-200 Saeta

Manufacturer: Hispano Aviación (design led by Willy Messerschmitt)
Country / decade: Spain, 1950–1960
Category: Military
Role: Twin-jet advanced trainer
First flight: 12 August 1955
Entered service: 1962, with the Spanish Air Force

Design and development

Messerschmitt's original concept for what became the Saeta began, in the early 1950s, as an ultra-light tailless delta fighter design rather than a conventional twin-jet trainer; that first attempt failed during glider testing and was abandoned, and only after Egypt separately acquired rights to develop the concept further did the aircraft evolve into the twin-engine trainer configuration that eventually entered Spanish and Egyptian service. The type's slow, decade-long path from failed prototype to operational trainer reflects how underfunded and technically constrained Spain's aircraft industry still was relative to the rest of Western Europe through the 1950s.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Spanish Air Force was the main operator; Egypt also bought the type and license-built 90 examples locally as the Helwan HA-200B Al-Kahira. An armed derivative, the HA-220 Super Saeta, later saw action against Polisario forces in the 1970s.

Notes

The Saeta was Spain's first jet-powered aircraft, designed with the direct involvement of German engineer Willy Messerschmitt, who had relocated to Spain after the Second World War — a striking illustration of postwar European aviation talent crossing borders.
