Spain · 1950–1960

Spain's aircraft industry operated under very different conditions from the rest of Western Europe in this decade. General Franco's regime pursued a policy of economic self-sufficiency (autarky) and remained diplomatically isolated through much of the early Cold War, excluded from NATO and from the postwar reconstruction aid that helped rebuild industry elsewhere. Construcciones Aeronáuticas SA (CASA), founded in 1923, had spent the 1940s building licensed versions of the German Messerschmitt Bf 109 for the Spanish Air Force.

Spain's first genuinely jet-powered aircraft, the Hispano HA-200 Saeta, first flew in 1955 — designed with the direct involvement of German engineer Willy Messerschmitt, who had relocated to Spain after the war when Allied restrictions barred him from aircraft work in Germany itself. It is a striking illustration of how European aviation talent moved across borders even during a period when Spain stood largely outside the continent's mainstream industrial and military alliances.

Compared to the jet fighters and airliners already flying in Britain, France, and soon West Germany, Spain's aviation industry in this decade remained modest in scale — a reflection of the country's broader international isolation as much as any lack of engineering ambition.
