Italy · 1950–1960

Italy's aircraft industry, like West Germany's, had to rebuild after wartime destruction and postwar restrictions, though Italy's constraints were lifted earlier and were less severe. Fiat's return to serious combat-aircraft design culminated in the G.91, which in 1953 won a NATO-wide competition (NBMR-1) for a standardised light strike fighter — a rare case of an Italian design being judged the best available to the whole alliance, ahead of entries from larger manufacturers elsewhere in Europe.

In practice, NATO-wide standardisation went only so far: despite the G.91's competition win, most member air forces still bought their own national designs, and only Italy, West Germany, and Portugal ultimately adopted the type in numbers. Even so, the win itself re-established Italian industry's credibility as a serious European supplier for the first time since the war.
