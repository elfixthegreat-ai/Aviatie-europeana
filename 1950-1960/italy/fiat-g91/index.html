Fiat G.91

Manufacturer: Fiat Aviazione (later Aeritalia)
Country / decade: Italy, 1950–1960
Category: Military
Role: Light tactical strike fighter and reconnaissance aircraft
First flight: 9 August 1956
Entered service: 1961, with the Italian Air Force

Design and development

The G.91's design drew heavily on the American F-86 Sabre, reflecting Fiat's wartime and postwar experience overhauling and studying Sabres supplied to Italy under NATO aid programmes, though the G.91 was a smaller, lighter aircraft purpose-built for the low-level strike role rather than air-to-air combat. Its NATO competition win in 1953 is sometimes cited as the high point of formal NATO fighter standardisation efforts during this period — a model rarely repeated with the same success in later decades.

Where it was used, and by whom

The Italian Air Force was the lead operator; the West German Luftwaffe became the largest single user, acquiring more than 300 aircraft (including German-built examples) from 1962; and the Portuguese Air Force flew the type extensively during Portugal's colonial wars in Africa.

Notes

The G.91 won a 1953 NATO competition (NBMR-1) for a standardised light strike fighter for allied air forces, though in the end only Italy, West Germany, and Portugal adopted it, illustrating the practical limits of NATO-wide standardisation in this period.
