1950–1960

The 1950s opened the jet age in European civil aviation and, at the same time, forced a near-total rebuild of several national aircraft industries after the devastation of the Second World War. Britain led the world into commercial jet travel with the de Havilland Comet in 1952, though a series of fatal structural failures in 1954 briefly ended that lead before a redesigned Comet 4 restored it in 1958. West Germany's aircraft industry, banned outright from building military aircraft under the postwar occupation statutes, was only permitted to resume production in 1955, and did so cautiously with small utility types. Elsewhere, national fighter programmes accelerated under Cold War and NATO pressure, and by the end of the decade several countries — Britain, France, Sweden, and Italy among them — were fielding domestically designed jet fighters.
