Netherlands · 1950–1960

Fokker had been one of Europe's most important aircraft manufacturers before the war, and the company used the 1950s to rebuild that reputation around a very different kind of aircraft: not a fighter or a prestige jet, but a rugged, unglamorous turboprop regional airliner, the F27 Friendship, first flown in 1955 and in service by 1958.

The F27 filled a market gap that larger manufacturers in Britain and France had mostly ignored, competing on operating economics for shorter regional routes rather than on speed or prestige. It would go on to become the best-selling European turboprop airliner of its era, laying the foundation for Fokker's reputation as a specialist in practical, short-field regional aircraft rather than a national flagship programme.
