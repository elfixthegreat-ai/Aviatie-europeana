Hawker Hunter

Manufacturer: Hawker Aircraft (later Hawker Siddeley)
Country / decade: United Kingdom, 1950–1960
Category: Military
Role: Day fighter, later fighter-bomber
First flight: 20 July 1951
Entered service: 1954, with the Royal Air Force

Design and development

The Hunter was developed to meet a 1946 Air Ministry requirement for a transonic day fighter, and its clean, swept-wing design proved exceptionally adaptable: variants ranged from pure interceptors to fighter-bombers and dedicated reconnaissance aircraft, and two-seat trainer versions remained in RAF use for advanced pilot training long after the single-seat fighters had been retired from frontline squadrons. Its combination of docile handling and strong performance made it a favourite demonstration and display aircraft well into the 1990s.

Where it was used, and by whom

The RAF flew the Hunter as its main frontline fighter through the late 1950s and then as its principal ground-attack aircraft through the 1960s, including combat over Suez and in Aden. It was widely exported to around two dozen air forces, including Switzerland, Sweden, Denmark, India, and the Lebanese Air Force, which kept it in service until 2014.

Notes

Around 1,972 Hunters were built across all marks, making it one of the most-produced British jet fighters. It set the world air speed record in September 1953 and became a favourite of RAF aerobatic display teams.
