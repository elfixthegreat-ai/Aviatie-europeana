Dassault Mystère IV

Manufacturer: Dassault Aviation
Country / decade: France, 1950–1960
Category: Military
Role: Transonic fighter-bomber
First flight: 28 September 1952
Entered service: April 1953, with the French Air Force

Design and development

The Mystère IV grew directly out of Dassault's earlier Mystère II, but its wing was so extensively redesigned — swept more sharply and built specifically around the American Hispano-Suiza-built Rolls-Royce Tay engine rather than the French engine used in its predecessor — that it amounted to a new aircraft under a shared name. Roughly two-thirds of its funding came through NATO's offshore military-aid programme, a reflection of how heavily American Cold War funding underwrote even notionally "national" European fighter programmes in the early 1950s.

Where it was used, and by whom

The French Air Force was the main operator, using the Mystère IV over Suez in 1956; the United States funded a large batch through NATO offshore procurement. India received around 110 new-built aircraft from 1957, and Israel received some 60 ex-French examples from 1956, using them in the 1956 Suez campaign and the 1967 Six-Day War.

Notes

The Mystère IV was France's first transonic fighter and the first Dassault design with a genuinely new, sharply swept wing rather than an adapted earlier airframe. Some 411 were built between 1953 and 1958, and the type remained in French service into the 1980s in a training role.
