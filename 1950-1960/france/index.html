France · 1950–1960

French industry spent the first half of the 1950s rebuilding a design and manufacturing base largely dismantled during the war and occupation. State ownership shaped much of that rebuilding: the nationalised firms created in 1936 were consolidated in 1957 into Sud Aviation, while Marcel Dassault's privately owned company pursued its own line of jet fighters, culminating in the Mystère series that armed the French Air Force through the decade.

The decade's clearest achievement was civil rather than military: Sud Aviation's Caravelle, which entered service in 1959 as the world's first airliner purpose-built for short and medium-haul routes. Notably, the Caravelle used British Rolls-Royce Avon engines — a reminder that even at the height of national "flag carrier" industrial pride, French designs still depended on components sourced from across the Channel.

By the end of the decade, French industry had gone from near-standstill to genuine international competitiveness, setting up the export ambitions that would define the 1960s.
